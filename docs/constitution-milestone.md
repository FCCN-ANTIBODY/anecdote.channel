# Milestone: CONSTITUTION — the labeler writes your law with you

> Status: **milestone vision, horizon** — the successor to [Milestone: Dark Mode](dark-mode.md).
> Dark Mode makes labeling and socialization *possible* (the signed snapshot, the sneakernet, the
> labeling commons, the contact-layer crank). CONSTITUTION is what those become when they turn
> inward: **your constitution, drafted with you by the labeler, out of what you did with its
> labels.** It realizes at the personal/offline grain what civic-node
> [`VISION.md`](https://github.com/FCCN-ANTIBODY/civic-node/blob/main/VISION.md) states at the
> constellation grain — governance is **authored**, evolves by **speciation, not amendment**, and is
> an **exit system**. Deferrals cite civic-node
> [`OPEN-QUESTIONS.md`](https://github.com/FCCN-ANTIBODY/civic-node/blob/main/OPEN-QUESTIONS.md)
> where a section exists; the rest are this milestone's items, named at the end.

## The thesis: something as dumb as the labeler can write your constitution

The real claim of this milestone is a provocation: **if the labeler can write an effective
constitution, then a constitution is not about the grace of our words, nor the motions of our
hands — it is about clear, objective statement of preference.** A tool with no eloquence and no
theatre, co-authoring your law out of what you accepted and rejected, is the proof. And that plain
statement of preference is not decoration: it is the **dropped-connection reason** — what a
data-pile, a Tell, or a list gives back when it denies someone who reached your contact or became
your neighbor. A question cannot resolve a judgment; a statement of preference can. So the
constitution written *in statements* is the machine-readable *why* at every accept/deny boundary.

## From assigned shapes to chosen ideas

You begin by making your constitution out of **shapes** — just the constituencies you are *in*,
which you do not choose so much as you are **assigned by location**. From there you reach the **idea
constituencies**, which you *do* choose. By the time you have mastered anecdote you will have formed
your constitution as a set of **acceptances of different labels from other people** — and picking
which shapes and which labels you prefer is like **adopting the accent you want**: the elegant
corollary to the constituencies you were simply handed. Expressing yourself through which Tells you
join and socialize with shapes the noise in your phone — but none of it makes you *stronger* with
the tool until it becomes your own authored law.

## Constitution as a rough assembly you draft into meaning

A constitution starts as a **rough assembly of all the tags you have ever filled** — and from that
raw pile you get to **draft what you think you mean**. Choosing the shapes and labels you prefer is
a rough critique of yourself that rewrites the constitution again; and **noticing that your
preferences have changed is a good thing to be conscious of** — if you come to like an old line
better, you can always change it back. Constitutions **wear masks and have voices** (the Atlas law
speaks in the first person — *"I offer a line, not a gate"*); yours is a document with a voice,
maintained, not a settings screen.

## Statements, not questions

Where anecdote's polls must be **questions** (write-ins always), the labeler writes **statements**
into your constitution — and only statements, so it stays plain. Not *"do I like this?"* but a
clear action word: **"I ban," "I filter," "I keep"** — saying what you do *not* want by stating what
you *do*, precisely so a constitution judgment can **resolve**. That is the difference between a
mood and a rule the machinery can read.

## Co-authorship, at the moment

The labeler does not write your law *for* you; it writes it **with** you, in the same gesture. It
**suggests a line and you write a line at the same time**, and you **pick or pass** — while it
proposes your oldest lines for removal-or-keep. Every single turn gives you a chance to write
something else in. Maintaining the document is the co-authored act, not a periodic chore: the law
grows as a side effect of using the labels, the way the labels themselves grow as a side effect of
reading.

## Commingled labelers — diversifying your DNA

When labelers commingle, each one **represents what you believe is the best labeling for
something** — so mixing them is **diversifying your DNA**. And when you are forced to turn the gears
in your Tells (§Q, §R), you **cascade forward things you have heard — not as things you would
endorse, but for others to see** whether they prefer a label a stranger used. Because **labels are
signed by their labelers** (§R's signature discipline, the jury-by-gesture attestation), even a
divergent label carries *who and what* made it: you can learn from it, decide for yourself which you
like better, and **if you like theirs, adopt it** — with their signature still on it. Attributed
cultural diffusion: you pass along without endorsing, and the next person chooses on the merits, not
on the source's authority.

## The baton: focus-lock as one-unit-of-work

An elegant piece falls out of a limitation: your own labeler **cannot label everything in your
inventory — it has cycles too.** That backlog is fine if you have friends (§R), and it is also the
key to **focus-locking the offline client**. There is a **baton being held**: so long as the user
holds it, the system does nothing. The **platform gesture is an interrupt** — the moment all the
platform layers converge and each gets to do **one unit of work**. So doing things on your phone is
what *gets things done*: consume a lot, turn a lot of gears, get your own inventory labeled. But
**not everyone reads as much as they witness** — which is exactly why we will want constitutions to
**trim what we even keep**. Even finely tuned, you might become a voracious reader, or none at all —
just a private messenger. The single-focus lock (Dark Mode #75), the crank (§Q/§R), and this
co-authorship are the same mechanism seen three ways: **the user's gesture is the clock, and every
layer ticks once per tick.**

## The socialization framework

Underneath it all: we are **free agents moving around who can rush together — and we already do.**
This milestone's mission is a **socialization framework** for that fact — one that guarantees the
people you encounter, *if they also had this tool*, could take it and go almost a **random
direction** with it. That randomness is the point: hand it to **all twenty-eight people you came
across** (more than you will ever scan QR codes with) and it proliferates on its own. The milestone
builds **strong socialization on the platform** and, in the same move, **your own constitution to
shape what you save** — because voracious or private, everyone needs a law that decides what is even
worth keeping.

## What Dark Mode must prepare

CONSTITUTION is reachable only if Dark Mode lands the substrate: **labels signed by their labelers**
(so divergent labels carry who/what made them), the contact-layer crank that cascades them (§R), the
commons that actually labels everything (#79/#80), and — named plainly — **a way to ensure the
socialization is possible in side channels.** Dark Mode labels the world and moves it around;
CONSTITUTION is where a person, out of that free association, drafts what they mean. Build the
signing and the side channels now, so the constitution has DNA to assemble from later.

## The items

Named so the vision does not evaporate; slicing into issues is a later planning round (as Dark
Mode's were).

1. **Labels signed by their labelers** — the attribution that makes divergent-label adoption
   possible (extends §R's signature discipline; the prerequisite Dark Mode must carry).
2. **Statements-not-questions constitution grammar** — the labeler writes plain preference
   statements (`I ban` / `I filter` / `I keep`) that a judgment can resolve; contrast with the
   questions-only poll rule.
3. **Co-authorship UI** — labeler-suggests-a-line / you-write-a-line, pick-or-pass, oldest-lines
   up for removal-or-keep; maintenance as a co-authored moment.
4. **Constitution from the rough assembly** — bootstrap a draft from all the tags you have filled;
   the self-critique loop that rewrites it as your preferences settle.
5. **Preference-drift awareness + revert** — notice when your preferences change; keep old versions
   so you can adopt the accent you want, or change it back.
6. **Commingled-DNA label adoption** — cascade-forward-without-endorsing; adopt a stranger's signed
   label on the merits.
7. **The baton / interrupt focus-lock** — one unit of work per platform gesture; unify with the
   single-focus lock (#75) and the crank (§Q/§R).
8. **Constitution as the dropped-connection reason** — the plain statement is the machine-readable
   *why* a pile/Tell/list denies a contact or neighbor.
9. **Side-channel socialization guarantee** — the free-agents-rushing-together framework; give-it-to-
   everyone proliferation, random-direction by design.
